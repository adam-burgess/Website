I, at 21 years of age, have decided to buy my first oscilloscope. Since, I am writing this a bit later I have bought my first DC power supply too.

First, I must admit, I'm not necessarily used to writing, it feels a bit unnatural to me, however, that's why I'm here and that is why I have created this site. To write. Also to gain a deeper, more instinctual understanding and appreciation of electronics. It's up for debate whether my lack of this 'instinctual understanding' is a result of the pressures of the tertiary education system or whether I simply didn't give myself the opportunity to learn. Most likely a combination of both as I would constantly distract myself whenever possible by talking to mates, gaming or whatever else university students do. 

I thought that this post, my first, would be as good as any to share my motivations for taking this project upon myself. My motivations for building a home electronics lab. It may be a bit of a convoluted story because I must confess I have wanted to do this for quite some time. 

I can think back to high school, where I was lucky enough to attend a school that offered an Engineering ATAR course. This sparked me with passion, or at least the most I could muster up as video game addicted teen. As a result of this passion, I remember telling myself that I was going to build a home lab. I _had_ to. I would spend day and night thinking about what would go in the lab, what equipment I would need and what morally ambiguous ways I would attain some of these pieces (I had no money). Surprisingly though, after all this thought and longing, nothing happened. This was actually the norm for teenager me, big thoughts but no action.

Again, a couple of years later, about two years into my degree , I felt that urge again. This time though I'm not sure if the urge was one born out of pure desire or a feeling of unease. This feeling of unease may sound a bit strange, but I wanted to get my life together, and '_locking in'_  on the engineering hustle seemed to be the path. However, I did in fact not lock in, at least not in an instant as I had hoped. So thus, as the grand idea slowly faded, I was left with no lab. 

Later, I stumbled across a youtuber, an older guy that goes by "[Imsai Guy](https://www.youtube.com/c/IMSAIGuy/videos)". In the world of modern Youtube, I must say that he seems to be a bit of an enigma. One watches his videos and can feel his passion for the work he does and the hobbies he partakes, while lacking the natural over-stimulation of normal youtubers nowadays. After browsing his uploads I came across a certain video that lingered in my mind, "[How I learned electronics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bgrubw6B_us)[ – Imsai Guy](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bgrubw6B_us)", where he described his oddly inquisitive and whimsical approach to learning decently advanced electronics concepts. To me – who had only learnt electronics in traditional tertiary education – this method of learning was so abstract, yet so compelling. I remember thinking that I would _love_ to have learnt the way he did, and I would love to have the passion the has. 

That leads to now. What actually motivated to finally get started? I would admit its same reasons as last time, but, I have changed a bit as a person. I am now someone that will take action, so as soon as I decided to start building a home lab, I instantly started looking. 

I found this analogue bwd525 Oscilloscope built in Melbourne on Facebook Marketplace, and it was only $150 (what a steal!). I have also bought a Power Designs Inc, analogue power supply, for only $80. 

As I start to play and experiment the way Imsai Guy has, I want to record my findings, technical or not. So over the next couple of months and especially after my exams, you can find me here, writing on things that matter to me. 

Next up is a spectrum analyzer and signal generator. Only if I can find them as analogue machines of course. 😛

BYE!
